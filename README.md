# Can Temu win in Africa beyond low prices?

**Temu entered South Africa, Nigeria and Morocco in 2024 on ultra-low prices. We analysed whether its China-to-door model can last in Africa, and designed an "Africa Operating System" to build an advantage beyond price.**

Strategy case · NUS Business School, BMS5120 Innovation & Strategies for Emerging Markets (Prof. Ishtiaq Mahmood) · Semester 2, 2025/26 · Group of 7

**My section:** the key strategic and organisational challenges, the diagnosis at the centre of the report.

## My diagnosis: three reasons the model can't last
**1 · A value proposition competitors can copy.** A value curve analysis showed Temu wins only on price and variety, both easy to replicate; Jumia matched factory-gate pricing through a sourcing office in Yiwu. African shoppers care most about payment flexibility, trust and reliable delivery, and prepaid-only checkout is a structural trust barrier in cash-based markets.

**2 · A delivery system built for the wrong market.** Delivery takes 15–20 days, against 24–48 hours from Jumia in Lagos and Abuja. 90.3% of South African Hellopeter reviewers gave Temu one star (Dec 2025), and many streets lack formal addresses. **The real competitor is informal retail**, around 85% of retail in these markets.

**3 · No capability to handle regulation.** The model relied on regulatory arbitrage. Nigeria's data protection regulator opened an investigation covering about 12.7M users (Feb 2026), and South Africa's National Consumer Commission opened its own probe. Jumia spent a decade building relationships with regulators.

## The team's recommendation: Africa OS (Four Actions Framework)
| Action | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Raise** | Payment flexibility (cash on delivery, M-Pesa) and a catalogue curated for local needs |
| **Create** | A Merchant Partner Network: informal shops become pickup points and cash-on-delivery agents, paid per parcel |
| **Reduce** | Aggressive discounting, replaced by prices set to each country's purchasing power |
| **Eliminate** | Prepaid-only checkout as the default, and a one-size-fits-all catalogue |

The report adds a 3-horizon roadmap, a risk mitigation plan and success metrics: repeat purchase rate, delivery time, contribution margin per order and customer acquisition cost.

## What I learned
- Low price is what customers *give up*, not a benefit they *receive*. A strategy needs something that can't be copied.
- In emerging markets, the competitor that matters is often informal and invisible in market-share data.
